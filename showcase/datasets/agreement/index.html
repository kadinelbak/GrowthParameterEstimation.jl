Does it match the papers?

Each dataset page opens with a coloured box comparing the showcase result with the source paper. This page collects them. The showcase applies one shared recipe to every dataset, so it re-analyses the data rather than reproducing each paper's own analysis; where the two differ, the box says why.

DatasetVerdictPaperThis showcase
Lewis lung carcinoma subcutaneous volumes⚠️ Partly matches the paperGompertz and power law gave the most parsimonious, identifiable description of the 20 lung tumours. Logistic and exponential were rejected. No model forecast well beyond the next measurement.Exponential-linear is the pick. Power law is a close second (ΔBIC 4.8), logistic third (5.8) and Gompertz well behind (ΔBIC 22.5).
LM2-4LUC+ breast xenograft volumes (vehicle)⚠️ Partly matches the paperFor the breast tumours, Gompertz and exponential-linear captured the dynamics best. Exponential-linear also forecast best, with prediction scores of 80% or more up to 12 days ahead.Gompertz is the pick, matching the paper. Exponential-linear is far behind (ΔBIC 68), and power law forecasts best (median error 16%).
Melanoma spheroid radius over time➖ Not directly comparable with the paperFitted a mechanistic spheroid model with necrotic and inhibited regions to outer and inner radii, comparing cell lines and seeding densities.Theta-logistic best describes outer radius over time.
PSA under intermittent androgen deprivation➖ Not directly comparable with the paperA clinical trial of intermittent androgen deprivation; the PSA data have since been modelled in many papers with different models, with no single published ranking to compare against.Gompertz growth with an on-therapy kill term is the clear pick (52 of 72 patients).
Breast xenografts with neoadjuvant sunitinib or axitinib✅ Matches the paperBenzekry et al. 2024 (PLoS Comput Biol 20:e1012088) modelled primary tumour growth as Gompertz-type with a sunitinib term proportional to drug concentration, and found a measurable effect on the primary tumour.Gompertz growth with a dose-proportional kill term beats Gompertz without a drug term by 45 BIC pooled over 99 mice.
NSCLC and bladder cancer target-lesion diameters under atezolizumab/docetaxel⚠️ Partly matches the paperCompared exponential, logistic, classic and general Bertalanffy, classic and general Gompertz. Gompertz gave the best balance of fit and parameters; general Bertalanffy and Gompertz predicted held-out scans best.With explicit treatment laws, logistic growth with a kill term is the pick and Gompertz with a kill term is 9 BIC behind; the two forecast equally well (median error 10.4% vs 10.5%).
PC9 parental and sublines, untreated➖ Not directly comparable with the paperIntroduced the drug-induced proliferation (DIP) rate: after a delay, treated populations grow or shrink exponentially, and the DIP rate is a better drug-response metric than an end-point count.Untreated wells only: plain exponential growth is the pick on 10 of 13 wells, consistent with the paper's assumption; the pooled pick is power law.
BT-474 growth at low seeding density✅ Matches the paperGrowth at low seeding density deviates from exponential growth; by BIC, a weak Allee effect on birth is more likely than a strong Allee effect, and both beat simple birth-death growth.Weak Allee growth is the pick by a wide margin (strong Allee is far behind) and wins on 28 of 48 wells.
H3122 parental vs alectinib-resistant co-culture➖ Not directly comparable with the paperMeasured the evolutionary game between alectinib-sensitive and resistant cells from their growth rates in co-culture, and showed that drug and fibroblasts change the game.Each population fitted on its own: theta-logistic is the pick.
