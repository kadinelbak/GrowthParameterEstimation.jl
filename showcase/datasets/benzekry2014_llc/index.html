Lewis lung carcinoma subcutaneous volumes

⚠️ Partly matches the paper

The paper found: Gompertz and power law gave the most parsimonious, identifiable description of the 20 lung tumours. Logistic and exponential were rejected. No model forecast well beyond the next measurement.

This page found: Exponential-linear is the pick. Power law is a close second (ΔBIC 4.8), logistic third (5.8) and Gompertz well behind (ΔBIC 22.5).

Why they differ / caveats: Power law agrees; Gompertz does not. The paper used a volume-dependent error model and its own initial condition. Here every curve starts from its first measurement with a 10% proportional error, which favours laws that keep growing.

Benzekry S et al. (2014) PLoS Comput Biol 10(8):e1003800; data Zenodo 3572401 · doi:10.5281/zenodo.3572401 · source: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.3572401 · licence: CC BY 4.0

Subcutaneous Lewis lung carcinoma tumours in 20 mice, measured with calipers and reported as volume (mm³), 6 to 12 measurements per mouse between days 4 and 22. Benzekry and colleagues used these curves to compare classic growth laws and found the Gompertz and power-law models gave the most parsimonious description that was also identifiable.

How this setup differs from the paper. Here every curve starts from its own first measurement and no injection volume is assumed, the error model is the showcase's proportional one, and selection uses the 2-BIC rule. These choices can change which model wins, so the ranking below is a re-analysis with the package, not a reproduction of the paper's numbers.

Package diagnostics

The package's built-in tools, run on every model in the panel on the same 12 evenly spaced curves, each starting from its showcase fit: fisher_information, profile_likelihood, bootstrap_joint_fit (residual, 40 resamples) and synthetic_recovery_benchmark (20 simulated data sets per curve), plus 5-fold cross-validation. Script: showcase/analyses/package_checks.jl.

The 10% proportional error sets the weights; its overall size is estimated from each fit's residuals (the same maximum-likelihood scale the BIC and the showcase profile use). Here the residual scatter is a median 1.3× the nominal 10%, so every interval and simulated data set is calibrated to the scatter the data really have.

✅ Fisher and profile intervals agree for the pick

For exponential linear, the Fisher (Wald) interval is within a factor of 1.5 of the profile interval for 18 of 24 parameter-curve pairs. Where they disagree, trust the profile and bootstrap intervals: the Fisher interval assumes the likelihood is quadratic around the estimate.

Profile interval closes: the 95% interval ends inside the parameter bounds on both sides. Cross-validation: the first point is the initial condition and stays in every training set; the others are dealt into 5 interleaved folds, each refitted with the showcase objective and predicted from the full curve, so this tests interpolation. A fold counts as converged when its fit reaches a weighted error per point within 4× the full-curve fit's (retried with up to 32 starts); folds that never do are not scored. The forecast check above tests extrapolation.

The package profile uses the package's default grid plus points at ±0.5 to ±4 Fisher standard errors around the estimate. profile_likelihood reports the outermost grid points inside the cutoff, which reads about 20% narrow at this spacing, so the widths here interpolate the crossing between grid points. The showcase profile searches outward and bisects the crossing, and uses the exact log-likelihood ratio n·log(wSSE/wSSE₀) rather than its quadratic form, so it reads slightly wider.
