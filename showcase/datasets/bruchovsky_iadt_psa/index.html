PSA under intermittent androgen deprivation

Bruchovsky N et al. (2006) Cancer 107:389-395; data as posted for Tanaka et al. · doi:10.1002/cncr.21989 · source: https://www.nicholasbruchovsky.com/clinicalResearch.html · licence: none stated (posted by trial lead for research use)

Serum PSA (ng/mL) in men with prostate cancer on intermittent androgen deprivation: therapy is given until PSA falls, stopped while PSA recovers, and restarted when it rises past a threshold, over several years and several cycles. These are the data Bruchovsky and colleagues posted from their phase II trial, used in many modelling papers on intermittent therapy.

Therapy switches on and off. The pipeline reads the on/off column of each visit and builds a 0/1 therapy schedule per patient (exposure_on_column), switching halfway between the last visit in one state and the first in the next. The panel holds one net exponential rate that ignores therapy, and exponential, logistic and Gompertz growth with a kill term that acts only while therapy is on.

Reading the result. Gompertz growth with an on-therapy kill term is the clear pick, both pooled and on 52 of 72 patients, and its three parameters have closed intervals on almost every patient. All of these laws describe a single population that responds the same way every cycle. They miss the patients whose PSA rises faster with each cycle (an emerging resistant population), which shows in the forecast check: predicting the last 30% of a multi-year series from the first 70% fails for most patients. Two-population models such as sensitive_resistant are the package's tool for that next step.
