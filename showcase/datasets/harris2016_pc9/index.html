PC9 parental and sublines, untreated

Harris LA et al. (2016) Nat Methods 13:497-500 · doi:10.1038/nmeth.3852 · source: https://github.com/QuLab-VU/DIPrateNatMeth2016 · licence: none stated (repository)

PC9 lung cancer cells (EGFR-mutant) and three single-cell-derived sublines (DS3, DS4, DS5), counted by automated imaging every few hours. Harris and colleagues used these plates to introduce the drug-induced proliferation (DIP) rate: after a short delay, treated populations settle into steady exponential growth or decline, and the slope of log counts is a better drug-response metric than an end-point count.

What this page fits. Only the untreated wells (parental growth curves and the DMSO controls of the dose plates, 13 curves with enough points). The erlotinib wells need a drug-response term and are set aside for a later treated-data page.

Reading the result. Per well the rule picks plain exponential growth on 10 of 13 curves, which is what the DIP idea assumes for untreated cells over this window. The dataset-level pick is power law only because the summed BIC is dominated by a few wells that slow near confluence; the per-curve counts are the better summary here.
