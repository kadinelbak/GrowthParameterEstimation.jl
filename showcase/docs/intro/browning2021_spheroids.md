Melanoma spheroids from two cell lines (WM793b and WM983b) seeded at four densities (1,250 to 10,000 cells), with outer radius (µm) measured from images for up to about three weeks; 170 spheroids in total. Browning and colleagues used these data to fit a mechanistic spheroid model with a necrotic core, rather than the single-population growth laws used here.

**What this page asks instead.** Treating outer radius as the growing quantity, which classic law describes each spheroid most economically? Outer radius rises quickly and then saturates, so laws with a flexible approach to the plateau do well. The theta-logistic law wins at the dataset level, with Bertalanffy close behind. Radius is not cell number, so the fitted rates describe how size changes, not how fast cells divide.
